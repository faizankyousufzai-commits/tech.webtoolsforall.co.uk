About Tech WebTools

Hi, I'm Faizan Khan Yousufzai, and I run Tech WebTools. I am an SEO specialist and web developer, and I spend my days building websites and fixing the small tech problems that slow people down. This blog is where I write down the fixes I explain most often, so you can follow them yourself.

I started this blog because most tech help online is too long, too technical, or full of ads before it reaches the answer. I wanted a place where a guide starts with the problem, gives you the steps in order, and tells you what to check before you delete anything or change a setting. No jargon, no hype, and no promise that one trick will fix every phone.

What you will find here is practical help for everyday tech. That means Android guides for slow phones and full storage, Windows guides for storage and speed, app guides for cleanup and updates, and safety guides that help you check a link before you click it. Each guide is written for a beginner, in plain English, with short sentences and clear steps.

I write each guide from real use. I check where the setting lives, what the screen looks like, and what can go wrong if you tap the wrong button. When a step deletes files or signs you out, I say so before the step, not after it. When a fix does not work for every phone or every version of Windows, I say that too, because phone makers change menus and names over time.

I do not pretend these guides replace professional help. If your phone has a hardware fault, if your data is lost, or if your work depends on a computer that will not start, a local repair shop or the official support team for your device is the right place to go. This blog helps with the common problems you can fix at home, and it tells you when to stop and ask for help.

Tech WebTools is a small, independent blog. There is no team behind it, no paid reviews, and no sponsored guides at launch. I add new guides as I have time to write and check them properly, and I update a guide when a reader reports that a step no longer matches their phone or computer. If you spot a mistake, please tell me through the contact page. I read every message and I fix what I can.

Thank you for reading. If a guide on this blog saves you a trip to a repair shop or an hour of frustration, it has done its job. Start with the blog, pick the problem that sounds like yours, and work through the steps in order.
