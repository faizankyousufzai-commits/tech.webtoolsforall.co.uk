How to Speed Up a Slow Android Phone

By Faizan Khan Yousufzai | Published 8 November 2026 | Last updated 8 November 2026 | 5 min read

A slow Android phone is frustrating. Apps take long to open, the keyboard lags, and the phone feels warm even when you are doing nothing. Most slow phones do not need a repair. They need a cleanup, because storage gets full, apps run in the background, and old temporary files build up over time.

This guide works through the safe fixes in order. Start at the top and test your phone after each step. The first two or three steps are often enough. Do not download a random speed booster app. Those apps often add more ads and more background work, and they can make the problem worse.

Before you start: keep the battery above 20 percent. Do not delete photos or files until you have checked that they are backed up or copied somewhere safe.

1. Restart your phone

Restarting is the simplest fix. When your phone stays on for days, apps and system processes keep running in the background. A restart clears temporary memory and stops apps that are stuck. Press and hold the power button, then tap Restart. If there is no restart option, choose Power off, wait 30 seconds, then turn it back on. Use the phone for a few minutes and notice if apps open faster.

2. Free up storage space

Android needs free space to work well. When storage is almost full, the phone has little room for temporary files and updates, so everything slows down. Open Settings, then Storage, and check how much space is free. Try to keep at least a few gigabytes free. Delete old videos you have already backed up, remove duplicate downloads, and delete screenshots you no longer need. A few large videos often free more space than a hundred small photos.

3. Clear the cache for problem apps

Cache is temporary data that apps save so they can open faster next time. Over time, that cache can grow large or become faulty, and the app slows down instead. Clearing the cache is safe. It does not delete your account, your photos, or your saved messages. Open Settings, then Apps, choose the slow app, tap Storage, then tap Clear cache. Do not tap Clear data unless you are sure, because that can sign you out and delete app settings.

4. Remove apps you do not use

Unused apps still take up storage, and some of them still run in the background or check for updates. Open Settings, then Apps, and look for apps you have not opened in months. Uninstall them one at a time and keep the apps you use every week. Be careful with system apps and apps from your bank. If you are not sure what an app does, leave it alone and search for its name first. You can install an app again later from the Play Store.

5. Update your apps and your system

Old apps can run slowly on a newer system, and old system software can have bugs that make the phone lag. Open the Play Store, tap your profile icon, then Manage apps and device, and update the apps you use most first. Then open Settings, then System, then System update, and check for a software update. Install updates over Wi-Fi when you can, and keep the phone charged during the update.

6. Turn off animations

Animations make the phone look smooth, but they add a small delay every time you open or close an app. On an older phone, turning them off can make the phone feel faster at once. This step is optional. Open Settings, then About phone, and tap the build number seven times to turn on developer options. Then open Developer options and set the three animation scales to Off or to 0.5x. You can turn them back on any time.

7. Check battery use and background apps

A hot phone and fast battery drain are clues that an app is working too hard in the background. Open Settings, then Battery, then Battery usage, and look at the apps at the top. If an app you barely use is near the top, open its settings and turn off background refresh, auto play, or location access. You can also restrict battery use for that app from the same screen. Do not restrict apps you need for calls, messages, alarms, or maps.

When a reset is the last option

If the phone is still very slow, a factory reset is the last option. A reset deletes all apps and data, so back up your photos, contacts, and messages first. Go to Settings, then System, then Reset options, and read the screen carefully before you confirm. After a reset, install only the apps you need. If the phone is still slow after a reset, the problem may be old hardware or a weak battery, and a repair shop can check it.

Most slow Android phones improve with a restart, more free space, and fewer background apps. Work through the steps in order, test as you go, and keep the fixes that help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will clearing cache delete my photos or messages?

No. Clearing cache removes temporary files only. Do not tap Clear data, because that can sign you out and delete app settings.

Do speed booster apps help an Android phone?

No. Most speed booster apps add ads and run in the background, which can slow the phone down more.

How much free storage should I keep?

Try to keep at least a few gigabytes free. When storage is almost full, updates and apps have little room to work.

Will a factory reset always fix a slow phone?

Not always. A reset helps when the problem is software or too many apps. If the phone is still slow, the cause may be old hardware or a weak battery.
