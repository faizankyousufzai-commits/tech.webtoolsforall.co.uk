How to Free Up Storage on Windows 11

By Faizan Khan Yousufzai | Published 8 November 2026 | Last updated 8 November 2026 | 5 min read

When Windows 11 runs low on storage, everything feels slower. Updates fail, apps take long to open, and you keep seeing low disk space warnings. The fix is not always a new hard drive. In many cases, a few gigabytes are hiding in temp files, old downloads, unused apps, and large videos you forgot about.

This guide shows you where the space goes and how to free it safely. Work through the steps in order, and check the free space after each step. Do not delete Windows system folders or files you do not recognise. If you are not sure about a file, leave it alone.

Before you start: back up photos and important documents first, or copy them to an external drive or cloud storage. Close the apps you are not using so Windows can clean up properly.

1. Check what is using your space

Open Settings, then System, then Storage. Windows shows a breakdown of apps, documents, pictures, videos, and temporary files. Wait a few seconds while Windows scans the drive. The largest categories are usually the best place to start. Click on a category to see more detail, such as your installed apps by size.

2. Turn on Storage Sense

Storage Sense is a built in Windows tool that cleans up automatically. It can delete temporary files and empty the Recycle Bin after a set time. On the Storage screen, turn on Storage Sense, then click it to open its settings. Be careful with the Downloads setting. If you keep important files in Downloads, set that option to Never, and clean Downloads by hand instead. Click Run Storage Sense now to do a first cleanup right away.

3. Uninstall apps you do not use

Apps and games are often the biggest use of space after videos. Open Settings, then Apps, then Installed apps. Sort the list by size, largest first, and look at the top. If a large game or app has not been opened in months, uninstalling it can free several gigabytes at once. Keep the apps you use every week, your browser, and your security software. If you are not sure what an app does, search for its name before you remove it.

4. Delete temporary files

Temporary files are files Windows and apps create while they work. Most of them are safe to delete. On the Storage screen, click Temporary files, wait for the scan, then tick the boxes for temporary files, Windows Update cleanup, and Recycle Bin contents. Read each item before you tick it. Do not tick Downloads unless you have checked that folder first. Click Remove files and let Windows finish.

5. Use OneDrive Files On-Demand

If you use OneDrive, your files may be taking up local space even when you rarely open them. Files On-Demand lets you see all your OneDrive files in File Explorer without keeping every file on the drive. Right click the OneDrive icon in the taskbar, open Settings, then Sync and backup, and turn on Files On-Demand. Then right click a large OneDrive folder in File Explorer and choose Free up space. The files stay in OneDrive and you can open them again any time you are online.

6. Move large files off the drive

Large videos, old installers, and photo collections add up fast. Open File Explorer, then the Documents, Pictures, Videos, and Downloads folders, and sort by size. A few large video files can free more space than a hundred small documents. Move the large files you want to keep to an external hard drive, a USB drive, or cloud storage. Check that the copy worked before you delete the original. Move only your own files, not program folders or Windows folders.

7. Run Disk Cleanup

Disk Cleanup is an older Windows tool, and it is still useful for old update files. Type Disk Cleanup in the Start search, open it, choose your main drive, usually C, and wait for the scan. Tick the boxes for the items you want to remove, then click OK. Click Clean up system files for a deeper scan that includes old Windows Update files. Only delete them if your current Windows version is working well and you do not plan to roll back.

8. Find your largest folders

After the steps above, check Storage again. If space is still low, look for the folders that are still large. In File Explorer, open a folder, click View, then Details, and sort by size. Check your user folders and your desktop first, because large files often sit on the desktop and are easy to miss. Delete only files you recognise and no longer need. Empty the Recycle Bin at the end, because deleted files stay there and still use space until the bin is emptied. Never delete the Windows folder or Program Files folders by hand.

If the drive is still almost full after all these steps, it may be time for a larger drive or an external drive for your media. A small drive fills up fast when photos and videos grow, and no cleanup can fix a drive that is simply too small for your files.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to delete temporary files on Windows 11?

Yes, when you use the Temporary files screen in Settings or Disk Cleanup. Do not tick Downloads unless you have checked that folder first.

Will Storage Sense delete my files?

Storage Sense deletes only temporary files and items you allow. Set Downloads to Never if you keep important files there.

How much free space should Windows 11 have?

Try to keep at least 10 to 20 gigabytes free on your main drive if you can. Windows needs free space for updates and temporary files.

Does emptying the Recycle Bin free up space?

Yes. Files in the Recycle Bin still use disk space until the bin is emptied. Right click the Recycle Bin and choose Empty Recycle Bin.
